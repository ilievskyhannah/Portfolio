01The problem

The brief was to grow the platform, but the team couldn't trust its own usage data, and users skipped posts without saying why.

The team trusted two surveys and session recordings. I'd seen on O.T AI how much interviews reveal that numbers don't, so I made the case for them and ran them with our UX designer.

What the interviews found, and what each finding led to

  1. 01Users skipped posts silently when the content didn't fit their values → Sharing preferences
  2. 02Users shared with no proof that any of it landed → My Impact, on the profile
  3. 03There was no way to report a fault, so problems were passed on by hand or lost → Reporting
  4. 04Failed shares were counted as successes for months → Reporting, so failures show up

02What I decided

01 · From the interviews

A personal profile for every user

Found
Users wanted posts that matched their beliefs, and proof that their sharing made a difference. With no profile, the platform knew nothing about them, so it could offer neither.
Chose
Sign-up, sharing preferences and a profile with impact, reporting and settings. It added a step before the first post, and we accepted that.

  3. 2Preferences

02 · For the data problem

Reporting from inside the profile

Found
Failed shares were counted as successes for months, and users had no way to report it.
Chose
Users can report a group or a technical issue from their profile, so the team hears about problems directly.

03 · From the usage data

A desktop version built for posting

Found
There was no desktop version, and posting was what users did most.
Chose
The app ran in the browser, so instead of stretching the phone layout, I designed posting for 7 screen sizes across tablet and desktop.

03What held

The profile went live after I left, with no changes to my design files. Desktop has since grown to about 30% of usage.

What stayed after I left, and what the numbers show now.

Held

The profile went live after I left, with no changes to my files. A third network later fit into the Switcher without a redesign.

Didn't

Desktop stayed under 5% in May 2025. Users arrived through a WhatsApp link, and that opens on the phone.

August 2026

Desktop has since grown to about 30% of usage, with 89% of PC sessions from returning users. It grew after I left, so I don't count it as my result.
